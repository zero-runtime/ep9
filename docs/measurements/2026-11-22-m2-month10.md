## R22: why the gap stays, and the decisions D1-D7

1. **C1 (about 60% of the need):** on x86, ep9 runs the same instruction count as node --jitless on TS (57.24 G against 57.26 G at `m9-p5`, 1 box) but 9.4% (jit) and 10.6% (jitless) more cycles: IPC 2.462 and 2.407 against 2.688.
2. On the Mac, ep9 runs about 16% fewer instructions than V8, and this hides its lower IPC. So the Mac passes and x86 does not.
3. **C2 (about 40%, the jit cell only):** at the LEVER-13 merge the jit binary became 5.5% and 6.1% slower than the jitless binary in the same rounds. X1 shows that both builds run the same handler bytes (599 of 599 symbols), so it is not codegen; code placement is the lead hypothesis, not proven.
4. **C3:** the levers kept the Ir meter after the instruction gap closed: from the month-8 end, x64 TS Ir fell 9.6%, but the box-neutral time moved only -0.7% (jit) and -8.3% (jitless).
5. **C4, C5:** the time meter was too coarse (one-box intervals 1.9-6.3%, levers 0.2-2%) and rationed (3 lever A/B boxes a month); records and plans took 45-50% of the month-10 tokens.
6. **C6:** 14 of 17 open gate cells are open by procedure, not by engine (busy shared Mac windows; a 66 h counted fuzz batch).

The decisions (orchestrator, under the owner order "decide yourself"; M6-ORCH-1261, as changed by M6-ORCH-1263 and -1267; plan v16 takes them):
